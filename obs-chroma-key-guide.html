OBS Green Screen (Chroma Key) Setup

NET KENYA Virtual Classroom — replace your physical green screen with any background image or video, then feed it into Jitsi as your camera.

Physical Green Screen → Webcam → OBS Studio → OBS Virtual Camera → Jitsi (8x8)

1 What You Need

Physical green screen

A green (or blue) fabric backdrop, painted wall, or collapsible green screen panel. Even lighting on it is critical — no wrinkles, no shadows.

Webcam

Any USB webcam (built-in laptop cam works). Position it so you fill most of the frame with the green screen behind you.

OBS Studio (free)

Download from obsproject.com. Windows, Mac, Linux. The Virtual Camera feature is built into OBS 28+.

2 Set Up OBS Scene

Add your webcam

In the Sources box (bottom), click + → Video Capture Device → select your webcam. You should see yourself with the green screen behind you.

Add your background image/video

Click + in Sources → Image (for a still image) or Media Source (for a looping video). Select your background file. Drag this source BELOW the webcam source in the list so it sits behind you.

Apply the Chroma Key filter

Right-click the webcam source in the list → Filters. Under Effect Filters, click + → Chroma Key. The green background should disappear immediately, revealing your background image behind you.

  • Key Color Type: Green (or pick "Custom" and use the eyedropper to click the green in your preview)
  • Similarity: Start at 400, adjust up/down until green is gone but your edges aren't eaten away
  • Smoothness: 80–120 (softens the edge)
  • Key Color Spill Reduction: 100–200 (removes green tint on your skin/hair)
  • Opacity: 100 (leave at 100)

3 Fine-Tune the Chroma Key

Most common problem: uneven lighting on the green screen. If parts of the green screen look darker or lighter, the single chroma key won't cleanly remove all of it. Fix the lighting first — two soft lights aimed at the green screen from 45° angles, one on each side.

Use the preview

Watch the OBS preview (not your raw camera). Adjust Similarity and Smoothness until:

  • Green is 100% gone (no green fringes)
  • Your hair, shoulders, edges are clean (no "bites" taken out of you)
  • No green spill on your skin or clothes

4 Start the Virtual Camera

Click "Start Virtual Camera"

In OBS, look at the Controls panel (bottom-right). Click Start Virtual Camera. The button will change to "Stop Virtual Camera" when active.

If you don't see the button: You're on an old OBS version. Update to OBS 28+ from obsproject.com. On Linux, you may need to install v4l2loopback kernel module first.

5 Select OBS Camera in Jitsi

Join your Jitsi class as usual

Open the Virtual Classroom in NET KENYA and join the meeting.

Switch the camera

In the Jitsi meeting, click the camera icon (or ⋯ → Settings → Devices) and select OBS Virtual Camera as your camera. Your Jitsi participants will now see you with the clean background — no green screen visible to them.

6 Quick Troubleshooting

Green still showing

Increase Similarity. Use the eyedropper to pick the exact green shade from the preview.

Edges of you are disappearing

Decrease Similarity. Your green screen may not be evenly lit — add more light to the dark spots.

Green tint on skin/hair

Increase Key Color Spill Reduction. Also move closer to the camera and further from the green screen.

OBS Virtual Camera not showing in Jitsi

Make sure you clicked Start Virtual Camera in OBS before joining Jitsi. Refresh Jitsi's device list.

Background image looks stretched

Right-click the image source in OBS → Transform → Fit to screen (or Stretch to screen). Use a 1920×1080 image for best results.

Summary

Once set up, your workflow for each class is: OBS open → Start Virtual Camera → join Jitsi in NET KENYA → select OBS Virtual Camera. The chroma key runs in OBS, so Jitsi just sees a clean feed. No Jitsi-side configuration needed.
